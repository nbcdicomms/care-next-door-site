About the film

A reclamation of Black care

The Care Next Door is a celebration of Black Family, Friend, and Neighbor care as a lineage of love, legacy, and community that has nurtured generations.

The story begins in pre-colonial Africa. It travels through centuries of resilience. It extends into the Afrofuturist future NBCDI is helping build, tracing the joy, ingenuity, and communal power that have always defined Black caregiving traditions.

In the world of Black Family, Friend, and Neighbor care, next door is never just an address. It is anywhere trust, community, and safety live. This film honors the caregivers who keep those spaces, and the children and families they hold.

FFN care is the most frequently used form of child care in the country, yet it remains largely absent from child care policy, public investment, and research. The Care Next Door was made to counter the idea that this care is lesser, invisible, or merely babysitting. It names FFN care as essential.

The Research

Built on Reimagining Child Care

NBCDI's Reimagining Child Care project responds to a research gap by learning directly from the experts: Black FFN caregivers and the parents who choose, trust, and depend on them.

Letter Writing

Caregivers wrote to an FFN ancestor and a future descendant, surfacing gratitude, wisdom, legacy, and love across generations.

Paint Party

Participants painted a bouquet illustrating their hopes for FFN care, revealing themes of dignity, safety, joy, and purpose.

Affirmation Boards

Images, colors, and quotes gave caregivers a way to express identity, values, and dreams that are sometimes hard to put into words.

What we learned

  • Black FFN caregiving is a source of joy, purpose, identity, and pride.
  • FFN care is relational and inherited. Many caregivers see themselves as part of a lineage of care.
  • FFN caregivers can experience isolation. Spaces for connection and recognition matter.
  • Families choose FFN care for its trust, cultural understanding, flexibility, and safety.
  • Policy and research must recognize, resource, and learn from Black FFN caregivers and families.
